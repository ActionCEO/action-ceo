Wall Street lost $3.2 trillion in a single session because investors started asking if all that AI spending will ever pay off. Bitcoin got dragged down with it, but some crypto infrastructure is showing exactly what the market now wants: proof.

## The AI trade gets a reality check

Investors are asking if AI infrastructure spending will produce returns quickly enough. Quickly enough is the key part. Most people agree AI will make money. The question is how long it takes to get your money back. [Watch at 4:52](?t=292)

Let me be clear: AI did not stop working. AI helped me build the graphics for this very show. The economics just matter a lot more now. Think of it as a chain. GPUs go into data centers. Data centers need power. Power needs cooling. And all of it gets paid for with debt. Even Blackstone is saying more tech fundraising is coming just to keep up with AI.

Anthropic is the perfect example. It suddenly has to defend a potential trillion dollar plus IPO valuation, and investors are saying the AI backlash could threaten the timing. These models are expensive to train and to run, so investors want to see margins before they pay that kind of price. Add the uncertainty after the midterms and you get a market asking a lot more questions. My take: Wall Street just remembered that AI has a balance sheet.

## Bitcoin below $76K and the liquidation domino

Bitcoin did not get a free pass. As of this show on Nov 6, 2026, it fell below roughly $76,000, the level I keep telling you to watch. About $1.26 billion in leveraged positions got liquidated in 24 hours. [Watch at 12:44](?t=764)

If you're new: liquidation is when you borrow money to make a bigger bet, the price moves too far against you, and the exchange force closes your position. You lose what you put in. When a lot of people get closed at once, that selling pushes the price lower, which liquidates more people. Domino effect. It is the easiest way to make a lot of money and the easiest way to lose it all, so proceed with caution.

Fun question for the week: is Bitcoin a tech stock or digital gold? I think it flips between the 2 depending on the season.

## RWA keeps moving fast

I had just come off a stream with metals.io about tokenized copper, which I also cover in [my Tezos and copper breakdown](/blog/tezos-copper-rwa-quantum-security). Copper is only 1 piece of the tokenization story. [Watch at 16:04](?t=964)

Samsung launched USDC inside the Samsung Wallet. Eligible US Galaxy users can buy, hold, send and receive it, running on Solana. That is roughly 82 million devices, and no separate crypto app needed. Mass adoption might look like nobody knowing they're using crypto. Nobody cares if there's a little guy inside the ATM, as long as the money comes out.

On the institutional side, Schroders, a trillion dollar plus asset manager, tokenized an actively managed emerging market bond strategy with Centrifuge on Solana. Tokenization is moving beyond cash and Treasury bills into real credit exposure, with more risk and complexity. Quick explainer: a tokenized fund is the same fund you'd buy through a broker, but your share is a token on a blockchain. It can settle faster and move more easily.

## DePIN that actually gets paid

DePIN is the opposite of AI right now. AI got handed the money first. DePIN has to prove somebody will pay for the infrastructure. GEODNET does. Your phone GPS gets you within a few feet. Tractors, drones, surveyors and robots need centimeter level precision, and GEODNET corrects that positioning data using stations run by people like me. [Watch at 22:54](?t=1374)

Full disclosure, I run GEODNET miners and just checked my wallet. It looked good. The numbers I shared: about 23,000 active devices across 145 countries, $99,000 in 30 days, and about $14.6 million in cumulative protocol revenue. Real devices, real customers, real revenue.

Silencio is turning phones into a data network AI companies can buy from. They have 2.5 million contributors across more than 180 countries, collecting real world voice and audio data. AI models struggle with accents, low resource languages and noisy environments, and that is exactly what they collect. A customer buys approved data and contributors get paid, which can be in USDC, with SLC incentives separate. Earning from real data sales is a lot healthier than earning only from token emissions.

## Akash moves to Solana

1 of my most popular videos ever was about Akash Network. Akash mainnet 19 was scheduled for the day of this show, and its marketplace and token infrastructure are headed to Solana. Helium made the same kind of move when it went from its own chain to Solana. [Watch at 27:49](?t=1669)

The bigger story: Akash is basically saying the blockchain is not the product. The compute is. If you run a pizza company, why spend your time building the cash register? Focus on the pizza and use somebody else's rails. GEODNET does the same thing on Polygon. This is not a downgrade. For AKT holders, the thing to watch is demand for compute, not which chain it sits on. I track it at stats.akash.network.
