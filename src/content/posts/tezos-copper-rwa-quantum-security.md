Right now, 25 tons of real copper are sitting in a warehouse in Hamburg, Germany, and if you're eligible you can own a piece of it onchain through Tezos. While a lot of crypto is still selling narratives, Tezos is building for markets outside the crypto bubble, and the real question is whether that turns into real adoption. I'll tell you what's launched, what's experimental and what's only planned, because that really matters.

## Metals.io and 25 tons of tokenized copper

Copper is in everything that's growing right now: electric cars, power grids, AI data centers. If it carries electricity, there's copper in it. At the end of October, a platform called Metals.io launched xCu, a copper token. The first batch was 25,000 tokens, each representing 1 kilogram of refined copper. That's about 25 tons, worth roughly $366,000 at launch. [Watch at 1:27](?t=87)

This is real metal: copper cathodes in a bonded warehouse in Hamburg. Not copper in the ground, and not a mining stock.

It was built by Trilitech, a London-based Tezos development company. A regulated UK company called Archax holds the copper as a trustee for the token holders. It lives on Etherlink, and the goal is to grow into millions of dollars of copper over the next year, depending on demand.

## What xCu holders actually own

This is where people get burned, so read the fine print. You own a share of that stored copper. Real ownership, not just a price bet. But getting it delivered isn't simple. You need an approved storage account, identity checks and a minimum size. The team told CoinDesk it's about 125 kilograms, but check the current terms. [Watch at 3:06](?t=186)

The token isn't pegged to the copper market, so it can drift a little above or below. There's a 1% yearly fee for holding it right now. And the blockchain proves who owns the token, but it can't inspect the warehouse. You're still trusting the custodian, the warehouse and the legal structure around it.

The team does have a track record. Their uranium token came out first, and this summer Kraken started offering it to eligible users in Europe. According to the Financial Times back in August, the platform has reportedly done around $24 million in trading since launch, with about 9,000 token holders. Copper is its 6th metal.

My take: this is what tokenization should look like. Real inventory, a named warehouse, a trustee, clear rules. It's boring in the best way possible. For a regular person, you get exposure to physical copper without futures contracts or a storage unit.

## Etherlink: is there a real economy yet

Etherlink is Tezos's network that speaks the same language as Ethereum. It's EVM compatible, so existing Ethereum apps and wallets can run on it without starting over. You can connect MetaMask right to the Metals.io platform, and the basic DeFi tools are already there. [Watch at 6:18](?t=378)

Now the honest part. The money in DeFi on Etherlink is still small, under $20 million by DefiLlama's count at the time of this video.

I don't need hype numbers. I want steady growth in trading volume, more real assets like copper in DeFi, and users who stick around. The rails are there. The traffic isn't yet.

## Quantum computers vs your crypto wallet

Here's the scary one. Your crypto wallet is protected by a digital signature, and a quantum computer could crack it one day and steal the funds. That's not happening today, but Google has set a 2029 deadline for its platforms to be quantum-safe. The big players are taking it seriously, so I would too. [Watch at 7:50](?t=470)

In September, Tezos engineers launched Quantumnet, an experimental test network for a quantum-safe version of Tezos. It doesn't just swap wallet keys. It upgrades the whole system, from user accounts to how validators agree on blocks. To be clear: it's experimental, mainnet isn't quantum proof today, and there's no timeline yet.

Tezos upgrades through onchain votes, more than 20 times without splitting into forks, so there's already a path when the time comes.

## Tezos X and the Ganesha upgrade

Tezos X lets Ethereum-style apps and Tezos-native apps call each other in the same transaction without a bridge. That's a big deal, because bridges are one of the most hacked things in crypto. And if any part of the transaction fails, the whole thing reverses. In August, an upgrade called Ganesha put that architecture live on Etherlink. [Watch at 9:08](?t=548)

The easy-to-use tools are still being built. But I like that the infrastructure is going up ahead of time, instead of "give us money and we'll build it later."
