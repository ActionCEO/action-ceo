PropBase lets you earn rental income from hotels starting with $100. I had Kevin Goos, founder and CEO of PropBase, back on to explain how tokenized real estate actually works, where the yield comes from, and why most real estate tokenization projects failed while PropBase kept building.

The timing was good too. The PROPS token was set to launch on Robinhood Chain on Oct 29, the day this went live.

## What PropBase is, in 1 pitch

I asked Kevin for the elevator pitch, and his is simple. PropBase is a real estate tokenization platform offering fractional ownership with a minimum investment of $100. You invest in a property, earn rental yield in proportion to how many of that property's tokens you hold, and you can buy and sell those tokens on PropBase. [Watch at 1:01](?t=61)

Kevin is American but lives in Thailand, and PropBase focuses on hospitality: resorts and hotels. They've tokenized parts of properties under brands like Wyndham, Ramada and Cassia by Banyan Group.

## Building through the bear market

I always say crypto is an infinite game. If you stick around, you tend to win. But sticking around during a bear isn't easy. Kevin's view is that the bear has actually been good for PropBase. They shipped their full product suite in 2025 and hit pretty much everything on their 2026 technical roadmap. [Watch at 2:24](?t=144)

When launch pressure eased, the team went back and cleaned things up: UI and UX work on older products, and V2s of some of their contracts. He sees tokenized real estate as a 10 to 20 year vision, not a 1-cycle project.

## Sticking with Aptos and going omnichain

Something I respect: PropBase didn't chase liquidity to whatever chain was hot. Kevin loves Aptos and the Move programming language, and he says the Aptos technical team has been phenomenal on support. [Watch at 6:38](?t=398)

So why add chains now? PropBase built its own bridge into its product suite on top of LayerZero infrastructure. That started with Base, then Ethereum, and now Robinhood Chain for the PROPS utility token, which is separate from the tokenized assets. Kevin estimates PROPS has around 60,000 to 65,000 holders across chains. Solana is on the roadmap, and he says they plan to land on maybe 5 or 6 chains in total, favoring big chains with good distribution. [Watch at 7:50](?t=470)

## Why most real estate tokenization failed

Real estate was the first RWA story people talked about, and for most projects it fizzled. Kevin's answer on why PropBase survived is passion and focus on what he knows. He remembers asking early US founders, "Who's going to find the tenants?" and hearing "We are." That means buying lawnmowers and becoming a real estate agent. [Watch at 13:07](?t=787)

PropBase's model avoids that. The assets are already built, operating and well managed, so investors don't deal with tenants or maintenance. He wants "progressive" assets people are proud to own, like busy hotels with great lobbies and restaurants, not a 25-year-old shopping mall. His point for builders: tokenize what you know.

PropBase also won Real Estate Tokenization Platform of the Year at TOKEN2049 through RWA Week. Kevin says part of the reason was solving secondary market liquidity: their assets typically see healthy trading within about a month of going live. [Watch at 17:07](?t=1027)

## Where the yield actually comes from

This is the question I care about most. Back in the last bull run, centralized yield platforms like Celsius paid yield and nobody really knew where it came from. With PropBase, Kevin says the yield comes from people staying at the hotels, through long-term leases, short-term leases or nightly stays. [Watch at 20:07](?t=1207)

On numbers, he said PropBase's website shows a range of 8 to 12%, with 12% at the very high end, and he considers 7 to 8% the more conservative end. He was also clear that PropBase does not guarantee the yield. If you want another example of a boring, well-structured RWA, look at what [Tezos is doing with tokenized copper](/blog/tezos-copper-rwa-quantum-security).

## The security conversation nobody wants to have

Security is the least popular topic on the internet and the most important. We recorded the same week as a big PHP-related exploit. Kevin says PropBase doesn't build in PHP and has little WordPress infrastructure, but he still jumped on his computer to check for vulnerabilities, especially around wallet connections. [Watch at 21:34](?t=1294)

Before any release, PropBase runs third-party audits, mostly with CertiK and sometimes Hacken, plus test coverage of around 96 to 97% of the code base across roughly 500 tests. He made the point that attackers go after the top crypto projects without security departments, and after projects that were built, handed over and left unmanaged while still holding TVL.

My favorite line: starting a DEX isn't like starting an electric car. It's like starting a 1969 Mustang. Lots of parts need to sync, but it can run for a long time. [Watch at 24:52](?t=1492)

## Borrowing against your real estate tokens

PropBase Lend is next. Kevin says it's inspired by Aave and Compound's open-source DeFi mechanics, adapted for PropBase investors who want cash without selling their asset. It's built for less volatile assets: he says real estate in Thailand trends up about 5% a year according to the Bank of Thailand. It also includes auto-compounding and sub-accounts, all non-custodial, and lets people provide USDC liquidity too. At recording, he was targeting a launch around mid-November. [Watch at 26:40](?t=1600)

PropBase is a gated platform with KYC and AML. Kevin said their last asset offering had 150 participants from about 27 countries.

## Advice for founders and everyday investors

For founders, Kevin's biggest warning is the never-ending pivot. Founders hit resistance, pivot, and then never stop pivoting. With a utility token, you're pivoting your community too, and that doesn't work. He also says you're running 2 businesses in 1: the product, and the token. Neglect the token and it dies. [Watch at 34:57](?t=2097)

For investors, look at the team. Does their track record match what they're building? He also says RWA builders who've been around tend to be good people, and he gave a shout out to the Brickken and Blocksquare teams. [Watch at 38:18](?t=2298)
