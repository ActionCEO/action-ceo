Tesla showed a Roadster prototype flying through the air, and Google says it cleared an important bottleneck in quantum computing. Both are exciting, but the real question is what's ready today, what's still a promise, and what any of it means for your crypto.

## Tesla's Roadster takes flight

The new Roadster prototype has cold gas thrusters that let it do brief airborne maneuvers. Tesla did it live at the SpaceX facility. When they opened the door, an Optimus robot was sitting in the seat, so it was remote controlled. Still pretty stinking impressive. [Watch at 4:24](?t=264)

It also uses fans to suck the car to the ground for grip, like those toy cars that drive on walls, just car sized. With the thrusters, you're talking 0 to 60 in about a second. And no, that's not CGI. That's a car doing a 360 in the air over a semi.

My favorite detail is the bottom line. The thrusters run on air tanks filled by an onboard compressor, so you don't fill up with any special gas. You can reserve one now: $5,000 down, then $45,000 more within 10 days. That doesn't mean you'll drive it anytime soon. A demonstration and a product you can use are different milestones.

## Google's quantum bottleneck

1 of the biggest bottlenecks in quantum computing is creating something called a magic state. A new Google Quantum AI paper shows magic state cultivation working on Google's Willow processor, with reports of about 40 times fewer errors in the experiment. [Watch at 11:08](?t=668)

The simple version: a quantum bit can be in more than 1 state at once. Like I'm holding a number that's a 1 and also a 3. The headline here is not speed, it's accuracy. Quantum computers make errors, and fixing them eats a lot of resources. This improves 1 of the most resource hungry steps. A useful computation needs a lot more than a big qubit count, the same way CPU cores have to actually perform.

Before anyone posts "Google just broke Bitcoin": it did not demonstrate recovering Bitcoin or Ethereum private keys. It's progress on building blocks, not a finished cryptographic attack. I went into quantum and crypto security more in [my Tezos and quantum security breakdown](/blog/tezos-copper-rwa-quantum-security).

## Bunker mode and the AI math debate

There's a separate debate about AI discovering new mathematics, and that's where the "bunker mode" phrase comes from. Shout out to Coin Bureau's October 13 newsletter for a level headed write up. [Watch at 16:26](?t=986)

Ethereum researcher Justin Drake argued on October 7 that AI assisted math discoveries could threaten the signature curves wallets use, earlier than expected. His timeline is his personal worst case, not an established deadline. A Coinbase cryptographer pushed back, saying there's no evidence the elliptic curve assumptions have been weakened. Vitalik took the possibility seriously while warning against rushed wallet migrations.

Anthropic also published cryptography research in July involving HAWK and reduced round versions of AES. Neither result shows ordinary Bitcoin or Ethereum wallet signatures are broken. Ethereum's own security roadmap says quantum computers can't break its cryptography today. My take is close to Vitalik's: prepare carefully and don't let headlines rush you. The first rule of scammers is to put a rush on it.

## Bitcoin after the inflation reports

For Bitcoin this week, the real question was inflation. September CPI came in at 3.7% year over year, with core at 2.6%. Gas rose 9.1% in the month alone and drove more than half of the monthly increase. Producer prices added a bit more pressure on Thursday. [Watch at 22:28](?t=1348)

As of this show on Oct 16, 2026, Bitcoin was at $82,711 and ETH at $2,480, as of 7:22 that morning. People ask for my Bitcoin number. It's $76,000, and we were still well above it. If we lose 76, I start to worry, because I think it can slide to 74, 72 and into the 60s. Crypto holding up after hot data is interesting, but it doesn't prove inflation stopped mattering.

## Follow the AI money

There's a difference between a model getting better and a business getting paid. Google's story this week is access. Its Argon model is out for developers, enterprises and paid consumer plans, and it's in the Gemini app. Honestly, it doesn't suck. Gemini had a long way to go, so I'm impressed. [Watch at 28:15](?t=1695)

Whatever model wins your subscription, it still needs chips. TSMC's third quarter revenue was $46.72 billion, with 50.9% growth in local currency and a 66.4% gross margin. Chips at 7 nanometers and below were 79% of wafer revenue. Management guided fourth quarter revenue to $52.3 billion to $54.3 billion. That's guidance, not a promise. With chip back orders already running into 2027, I'd rather own the company printing the chips than the app.

## Where is the actual yield

Citadel bought 2 year US Treasury notes through BNY on Tradeweb, with tokenization making it possible. BitMine reported just over 6 million ETH held as of October 11, more than 5 million staked, and a 2.61% 7 day staking yield, about $336 million annualized. [Watch at 51:43](?t=3103)

Then DeFi Development boosted a preferred dividend to 14% as it pushes to grow its Solana holdings, effective November 2. Hypothetically, a $1.40 dividend on a $10 share is 14%. At $9 it's about 15.6%. At $11 it's about 12.7%. But if that $10 share falls to $8, the $2 loss is bigger than a year's dividend. A high preferred dividend is a corporate financing obligation, not proof the network pays 14%.

My 5 questions for any yield: who pays me and why, am I getting cash flow or temporary incentives, what happens to my principal in a bad market, can I exit when I need to, and does it still make sense after fees and dilution.
