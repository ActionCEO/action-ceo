This week the hype started breaking. Nvidia lost its founder, Bitcoin fell below $70,000, and Microsoft 365 went down for a day, and each one tests the same thing: what survives when the market stops paying for the story.

## Nvidia without Jensen

Jensen Huang resigned from Nvidia on November 12, effective immediately, and he is off the board too. The CFO is stepping in as interim CEO while keeping the CFO job, and the board named its first ever chair. [Watch at 3:00](?t=180)

The personal side is all over the headlines. He admitted to an affair, and according to the New York Times, US officials are looking at possible ties to Chinese intelligence. China calls it a smear, and nobody has been charged. So we watch.

The business question is bigger. How much of the AI story was built around 1 guy in a leather jacket? Key person risk is like a restaurant where the chef is also the brand. The kitchen still works after the chef leaves, but confidence gets tested. A boardroom change does not switch off installed GPUs. What I want to see is orders, execution and customer spending. Nvidia reports earnings on Tuesday, and that is the first real test without him.

## Bitcoin below $70K

As of this show on Nov 13, 2026, Bitcoin broke below $70,000 and Ethereum broke below $2,000. I have been talking about $76,000 for a while. Once that level went, I expected lower. [Watch at 7:09](?t=429)

I am not blaming Jensen for crypto. But when money gets pulled out of Wall Street, red candles show up everywhere, crypto included. Round numbers shape headlines and trader behavior. They are not automatic floors.

The real problem is leverage. Leverage means borrowed exposure. If a position drops far enough, the platform closes it for you. That forced selling pushes the price lower, which triggers more exits. Also remember a token, a network and its fees are 3 different things. A cheaper token is not a better business, and a falling chart does not mean nobody uses the network.

## Microsoft 365 went down

On November 12, Microsoft 365 went down: Outlook, Teams and Copilot, not just 1 app. Engineers were still stabilizing things the next morning, and the status page still showed degraded service during the show. Microsoft says it was not a cyber attack. It points to a failing automated health check and heavy load. [Watch at 10:29](?t=629)

I talked to people who basically lost their workday. That is concentration risk leaving the spreadsheet and landing on your calendar. You can have 5 apps and still have 1 giant dependency. 5 stores sharing 1 power supply are not 5 backups.

Resilience means independent backups, tested recovery, a backup way to communicate, and someone accountable. Vibe coders, this one is for you. If Microsoft can have a day like this, nothing stops your AI agent from making a mistake and taking down the whole stack you vibe coded. A centralized provider having a bad day also does not prove every decentralized option is ready. Show me the uptime.

## RWAs and stablecoins keep building

While everyone stares at red candles, the institutions keep building. Real world assets, or RWAs, are real things represented on a blockchain. Start with ownership and legal rights, not token price. [Watch at 15:10](?t=910)

With a real RWA, I can lean on 2 prices. Take the tokenized copper that just launched on metals.io. If I need to move fast, I use the token price. If I hold enough, I can redeem the actual commodity and sell it at market. That is much less risk than relying on 1 price. I covered the metals.io copper story in [my Tezos and tokenized copper breakdown](/blog/tezos-copper-rwa-quantum-security).

The DTCC, the back office that settles most US stock trades, plans to connect its tokenization service to Solana. The service launched October 28, and Solana is expected in the second half of 2027. Anchorage also said Visa and eligible partners can use Western Union's stablecoin to settle Visa obligations. That is institutional settlement, not every shopper paying in stablecoins. A new settlement option is not proven volume. I want repeat counterparties and real economics.

## DePIN has to show the customer

DePIN is decentralized physical infrastructure. People supply compute, connectivity or data, software coordinates it, and tokens may reward participation. A network full of suppliers is not a network full of customers. [Watch at 21:01](?t=1261)

So ask: who buys, what unit do they buy, do they come back, and what happens when incentives shrink? For io.net, don't show me how many GPUs could work. Show me how much useful work customers pay to finish. Render turns 3D scenes into finished frames on other people's GPUs, and artists pay for that time. Helium does wireless, including carrier Wi-Fi offload. Coverage is the offer. Traffic someone pays for is the business test.

GEODNET is still my favorite, and I went deeper on it [last week](/blog/action-items-ai-crash-bitcoin-depin). Remember that a signed contract, recognized revenue and collected cash are 3 different stages. A viewer also asked about Deeper Network. I still run their devices, and Deeper is how I first understood DePIN. An update is on my list.

## Compute futures, quick hits and what I'm watching

The wild card: compute futures. A futures contract ties an agreement to a future price. Compute futures would track a GPU rental price index. Say a company plans to rent 10,000 GPU hours at $1 an hour. A hedge could offset some of the pain if prices rise, but only if the benchmark tracks the hardware they actually need. [Watch at 25:49](?t=1549)

Quick hits. Coinbase got final approval from the OCC for its national trust company, a custody setup, not a retail bank. Figment says it supports staking on BNY's custody platform for eligible institutions. SoFi USD went live on Polygon, joining Ethereum and Solana. It is a stablecoin, not an insured deposit.

Next I'm watching Nvidia's earnings and the weekly crypto candle on Sunday. The weekly red candles keep getting bigger, which is not a good sign. My homework for you: pick 1 project, find the customer, and look for proof the payment is real and repeatable. An announcement is the start of the measurement, not the end. If nobody pays, you don't have a business. You have a hobby.
