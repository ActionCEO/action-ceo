The number the whole AI trade was running on turned out to be soft. OpenAI's annualized revenue looks closer to $50 billion than $70 billion, and while that spooked Wall Street, the debt sitting under the AI boom is the part that actually worries me.

## The $20 billion number

Reports since August had OpenAI's run rate at about $68 to $70 billion. Then the Financial Times put it at about $50 billion as of September. The Nasdaq fell 1.25% and chips got hit hard. 1 report about 1 private company knocked a whole sector down in a day. That should not be happening. [Watch at 0:13](?t=13)

To be clear, OpenAI didn't shrink. So why the gap? Anthropic counts the full value of sales through AWS and Google Cloud. OpenAI reports net and leaves partner revenue out. A source told SiliconANGLE the $68 billion figure included gross partner revenue and the $50 billion is net. Anthropic has reported a $65 billion run rate. Both numbers are reports, not filings. [Watch at 22:51](?t=1371)

Also watch the timing. OpenAI started rolling out GPT-6 the day before it pitched 1.2 billion weekly ChatGPT users. Product day and number day, bundled together so it all looks better. Read between the lines.

## Bitcoin's floor was leverage

As of this show on Oct 9, 2026, Bitcoin was sitting at $82,750 after a Thursday low of about $80,300. It held $80,000, barely. Around $1.2 billion got liquidated in 24 hours, more than $1 billion of it longs. [Watch at 5:54](?t=354)

Ethereum had $356 million liquidated versus Bitcoin's $298 million. Adjusted for the size of each market, ETH got hit about 6 times harder. The biggest single wipeout was almost $20 million on 1 ETH position on Hyperliquid. For the week, BTC was down about 4% and ETH about 9%. Bitcoin sat about 35% under its $126,000 record.

What did the damage was oil and bond yields. The 10 year Treasury hit about 5.3%, the highest close since 2002. Then Trump posted that the US won't attack Iran before the November 3 midterms, and Bitcoin bounced to $82,000. 1 post. Bitcoin is a macro asset now. If you're not watching oil and the 10 year, you're trading blind.

And the 4chan prophet: a December 2023 post called the next top for October 2025, and Bitcoin hit $126,000 on October 6. The same timing pointed to a bottom this week. It didn't land. Out of 1,000 guys who were wrong, nobody screenshots anyone but the 1 who got it right. Don't make that your trading plan.

## Bunker mode: can AI break crypto's math

The scariest crypto headline this week was "bunker mode." Ethereum researcher Justin Drake told holders to plan to move funds to addresses whose public keys were never exposed. His worry is AI math progress making it more plausible to break Bitcoin and ETH wallet signatures, in months, not years. He pointed to OpenAI releasing 722 AI generated math results this week. [Watch at 18:21](?t=1101)

There was pushback. Vitalik said it's serious but he's not ready to scramble. Others called it the FUD of the century. Meanwhile projects like Algorand and Starknet are leaning into quantum resistance. I followed up on this debate [the next week](/blog/tesla-roadster-google-quantum-crypto).

## The AI debt binge

Revenue is the story they sell you. Debt decides who survives. Broadcom is reportedly seeking $50 billion in financing for OpenAI custom chips. SpaceX is looking for about $40 billion for Nvidia hardware. Oracle is in talks with Apollo and Goldman on a deal where investors buy the chips and lease them to Oracle. These are reported talks, not closed deals. [Watch at 25:52](?t=1552)

Oracle's debt went from $99.5 billion in November 2024 to $169.1 billion by August 2026, and free cash flow over the last 4 quarters is negative $28.7 billion. That's how much cash this buildout eats.

So who gets paid? Nvidia already got paid when the chips shipped. Private credit like Apollo collects fees and interest on chip backed loans. The risk sits with whoever holds the debt when the GPU loses value, and anyone who has mined crypto knows GPUs lose value fast. Infrastructure is great, but it needs users. Without a 2 sided market, you're setting yourself up for failure.

## Where your stablecoin yield comes from

3% or 4% on your stablecoins isn't magic. Coinbase pays 3.6% on business USDC balances. That money is interest Circle earns on the T-bills and cash backing USDC, shared with Coinbase under their revenue deal. With fed funds at 3.75% to 4% after September's hike, Circle and Coinbase split what's left. Stablecoin yield is T-bill interest with a middleman taking a cut. [Watch at 35:10](?t=2110)

That could change by January. The GENIUS Act bars issuers from paying holders interest, effective January 18 at the latest. A proposed OCC rule would presume a violation if yield gets routed through affiliates or partners. That's the exchange gray zone. Rewards could be cut, restructured or turned into points.

USDT is getting squeezed too. The EU's securities regulator gave licensed platforms about 3 months to stop offering unauthorized stablecoins, and USDT is the big one affected. A US senator also sent a letter to Cantor Fitzgerald, which holds USDT reserves, asking about sanctions and safeguards. It's a letter, not a subpoena.

The bullish counterweight: Samsung Wallet is adding USDC for eligible US Galaxy users, about 82 million compatible devices, with sends to bank accounts in 60 plus countries. It's custodial, with Bastion as custodian and Coinbase as subcustodian. And Canton's September recap showed the London Stock Exchange Group joining as a super validator. Canton works with Genzio, and I broke the network down in [my Canton explainer](/blog/canton-network-wall-street-blockchain).
