Uber just tested moving money onchain with HSBC and Franklin Templeton, and it didn't do it on Ethereum or Solana. It did it on Canton. If you've never heard of Canton, that's kind of the point: it's built for Wall Street, and October was a packed month.

## Why banks like Canton

Quick version if you're new. Canton is a public blockchain like the others, but it's built for regulated finance. That means banks, exchanges and asset managers, the people who move big money around, are interested in it. The main reason is privacy. On most chains, everyone gets to see everything. On Canton, only the parties in a deal can see the details. [Watch at 0:20](?t=20)

Think about it from a bank's side. You don't want a competitor watching your positions in real time. With Canton, they can't.

## The DTCC goes live on Canton

Let me start with the biggest name. If you've ever held a U.S. stock, it went through the DTCC. They clear and settle basically every stock trade in the U.S. They sit in the middle of the entire system. And the DTCC tokenization service just went live on Canton. They're minting tokenized versions of Russell 1000 securities, major index ETFs and U.S. Treasuries. [Watch at 1:19](?t=79)

If tokenization sounds like jargon, here's what it means. You take an asset that already exists, like a stock, a bond or a treasury, and you put a representation of it onchain. It's no different than the actual stock, but now it's recorded somewhere immutable. The point: it moves fast, settles faster and can be used as collateral without old back office systems.

My take: this is not a crypto startup experimenting. When the DTCC puts something onchain, that's the plumbing moving. And these aren't niche assets. The Russell 1000 is basically the biggest 1,000 companies in the United States.

## Uber, Fairmint and Litecoin

Uber completed a pilot for onchain liquidity and settlement. The partners were HSBC, Franklin Templeton, Tradeweb and Fireblocks. Why would Uber care? Uber moves money to drivers and partners in a lot of different countries. Faster settlement means less cash sitting idle in transit, and money sitting around is basically money being lost. [Watch at 3:47](?t=227)

To be fair, this was a pilot, not a production rollout. But a pilot with names like these is a big signal.

Fairmint is putting official shareholder records onchain. A transfer agent keeps the official record of who owns a company's shares. Usually that record sits in private databases with no transparency. Fairmint now does that job with the official record kept onchain using Open Cap Table Protocol. So the cap table lives on the blockchain and updates in real time.

And for the retail crowd, Litecoin is coming to Canton as cLTC, with support from the Litecoin Foundation.

This is what I wanted to see from tokenization. Not another token. Real securities and real settlement moving onto crypto rails. For a smaller-scale version of the same idea, my [PropBase interview on tokenized hotel income](/blog/propbase-hotel-rental-income-rwa) is a good companion.

## CIP-0129: who pays for the network

Every network has a bill. Canton just decided every synchronizer pays it the same way. CIP-0129, called Universal Burning and Synchronizer Pricing, just got approved. [Watch at 5:04](?t=304)

A synchronizer is the part of Canton's network that coordinates transactions between parties. Think of it as a traffic controller. Canton Coin already gets burned for traffic on the main global synchronizer. This change extends that to every production synchronizer. Phase 1 is targeted for Q1 2027, and since it's already November, that's right around the corner.

If you're new to burns: when a token is burned, it's permanently removed from the supply. Gone. So more usage means more Canton Coin burned, and the token gets directly tied to how much the network is used.

Here's the part people skip. New Canton Coin is minted as rewards for running the network and building apps on it. So the burn is fighting new issuance. The question is which side wins, and whether there's enough traffic to make it worthwhile.

## Burn is not yield

I like it when token demand is tied to real usage. But burn is not yield. Nobody is paying you to hold CC. I wouldn't buy a token just because it has a burn mechanism. I want to see the usage numbers first. [Watch at 7:24](?t=444)

So if you're holding CC, the thing to track isn't just a price chart. It's how much traffic these synchronizers are actually paying for.

## Going country by country

Canton isn't chasing crypto Twitter. It's chasing depositories and market infrastructure. Why depositories? Because that's where securities actually live. Win the depository and you win everything built on top of it. And the foundation's members already include huge names like Goldman Sachs and HSBC. This is a pattern, not a one-off. [Watch at 8:35](?t=515)

In Taiwan, Canton signed an agreement with the Institute for Information Industry, a major tech research institute, which brought Taiwan's central securities depository on as a member. In Singapore, during Token2049, they ran Canton Forum and Canton Connect, and the team was on stage across that week. They also launched Canton Partners, a new global partnership program.

On the builder side, a new version of the Dev Hub is live with an AI-first way to explore Canton's improvement proposals and developer tools. This is the part I'd watch. If it gets easier to build on Canton, the usage question starts answering itself. And yes, there's official merch now, which sounds silly, but it's a signal that people want to be part of this.

## What I'm watching next

My take on October: Canton is quietly becoming the chain banks actually use. The DTCC integration is real infrastructure, live today. The Uber pilot is a real test, not hype. The burn ties the token to usage, but it's targeted for early 2027, and burn isn't yield. [Watch at 11:03](?t=663)

My watch list: do these pilots turn into production, do the usage numbers show up, and does phase 1 of the burn land on schedule.
