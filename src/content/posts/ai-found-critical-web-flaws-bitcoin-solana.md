AI helped researchers find serious flaws in PHP, the software behind a huge share of websites, and the fixes are already out. That is a real example of AI finding the cracks, and it raises a bigger question this week: what actually changed, who benefits, and where does the return come from.

## AI is finding the cracks in PHP

Last week I talked about what AI might eventually do to security. This week we got a full example. PHP pushed out emergency security updates on October 21 for not 1 but 2 critical vulnerabilities that let attackers control affected servers remotely. That is about as bad as it gets. [Watch at 5:06](?t=306)

Here's why crypto people should care. The most common thing we do on a website is click "connect wallet." If the server behind that site is compromised, an attacker doesn't need to change much. They just swap the wallet connection, and now you're interacting with their contract instead of the company's. That's why I'm holding off on clicking connect anywhere for a few days.

The patch is for PHP 8. I know at least 3 companies I've worked with that are still on PHP 7, and they're freaking out, because for them it's not a quick patch. It's a full upgrade.

The good news is the response. OpenAI put up a page about coordinated disclosure of critical vulnerabilities as agents uncover them, and Anthropic says its bots will run continuous security checks on your environment. Good. That should be the bare minimum. The only real counter to AI looking for holes is AI doing the opposite work. I also talked with Kevin, the CEO of PropBase, right before the show. He pulled up logs showing people trying to breach their servers all the time. Web3 gets hit constantly because it's easier to pull value out.

## Meta's AI bill

The grass is always greener on the other side. People forget the neighbor is paying a lot to irrigate that lawn. Meta's third quarter 2026 revenue came in at $64.6 billion, up 26% year over year. Great. But costs and expenses grew 68%. [Watch at 16:27](?t=987)

Capital expenditures, including finance lease principal, came out to $34.9 billion, and reported cash flow was negative $2.12 billion. Meta is giving Muse away free to win users, the same way Apple got into schools early. Once you like how an AI model talks to you, you stick with it. That's the bet. And I'll use Muse as much as I can because it's free. But the release doesn't isolate a standalone AI profit figure, and that's what I want to see. A strong revenue quarter and heavy spending can both be true. Shareholders are the ones who pay the bill if it doesn't work out.

## Bitcoin ETF money is back

Bitcoin has been holding its own. As of this show on Oct 23, 2026, it was near $81,700, and I'm still watching that $76,000 level from last week. Bitcoin ETFs pulled in just over $1 billion in 3 days. [Watch at 23:13](?t=1393)

That's encouraging, but it might not be enough. Using CoinDesk's chart, we haven't had many $1 billion plus days lately. In 2024 there was 1 in the first half and 3 in the second half. In 2025 there were 2. What's missing is consistency, like retirement funds buying Bitcoin on a schedule. A good 3 day number is useful. It is not a reason to stop managing risk.

## Solana's Alpenglow and JPM Coin on Ethereum

Solana switched on Alpenglow, a change to how the network reaches agreement. The upgrade page shows it live on mainnet, activated at epoch 1064 around October 21. It targets roughly 150 milliseconds of finality, versus about 12.8 seconds before. [Watch at 35:42](?t=2142)

Finality is the point where a transaction is settled by consensus and set in stone. It is different from your wallet just showing it. 150 milliseconds is a target, not a guarantee, but anything under a second is phenomenal. It also changes validator economics: official materials describe a 0.8 SOL validator admission fee per epoch, replacing the old voting transaction fees. For stakers, the question is whether costs, commissions and reliability change. Token price still depends on demand.

Meanwhile JPMorgan brought JPM Coin, ticker JPMD, to Ethereum. It's a US dollar deposit token for institutional clients, not a retail coin. Know the difference: a payment stablecoin has a reserve and redemption framework, a tokenized deposit represents a bank deposit relationship, and a tokenized fund is an interest in a portfolio. I want to know who owes the money and what rights I actually have.

## Where is the actual yield

The OCC issued final GENIUS Act rules for the entities under its jurisdiction, with an effective date of January 18, 2027. They cover reserves, redemption, risk management, custody, reporting and capital. The rule keeps the restriction on issuers paying interest just for holding a payment stablecoin, and the OCC declined to presume older reward programs are compliant. [Watch at 45:03](?t=2703)

I wouldn't build an income plan around a rate just because an app has offered it for a while. We saw FTX and Celsius. Even when a reward is allowed, ask where the money comes from. CoinDesk, citing the Financial Times, reported crypto lending vault deposits topped $10 billion, up from about $4 billion at the start of 2025. Curators pick strategies, set risk limits and collect fees. Growing deposits do not certify a strategy as safe. I want to know who manages it and what happens when collateral has a bad day.

## Use AI to check an income claim

I want AI to work for me, not to make my decisions. Always ask for the return source, the borrowers or cash generating asset, the fees, withdrawal conditions and failure scenarios. Then have the model separate the product's own claims from facts it can verify, and open the sources yourself. [Watch at 52:25](?t=3145)

Here's the prompt I use. Part 1: "Review this income product for me. Explain who pays the return and why. Identify fees, withdrawal limits, and what can cause principal loss." Part 2: "Separate verified facts from marketing claims and unknowns. Use dated primary sources. Do not fill missing terms with assumptions." Try it on an RWA platform like PropBase, which I covered in [my interview with their CEO](/blog/propbase-hotel-rental-income-rwa). AI won't be wiser than you. It will be faster.
