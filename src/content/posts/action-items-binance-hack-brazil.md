Binance took an estimated $6.3 billion hit in what appears to be the biggest crypto heist on record, and my home country of Brazil is in an election crisis. Crypto just got punched in the face, but some builders are shipping real products anyway, and that matters just as much.

## What actually happened inside Binance

First, Bitcoin didn't break. Ethereum didn't stop working. This appears to have started with access inside Binance itself. Withdrawals were paused and about $3 billion connected to the attack was frozen. [Watch at 4:08](?t=248)

Think of Binance like a crypto bank. You give them your crypto and they control the wallets that hold it. That is centralized custody. Self-custody is the opposite: you hold your own keys.

Binance first said a DevOps engineer's laptop appears to have been compromised. By the morning of this show, reporting said investigators had found additional breach points. So don't sum this up as "1 laptop lost $6 billion." The laptop looks like an entry point, maybe not the only one.

Here is the key distinction. The thief apparently didn't need the master safe key. They got access to the system that stamps withdrawals as already approved. Reuters' explainer mentions EIP-7702, an Ethereum feature that gives a normal wallet more programmable abilities. The attacker appears to have gotten valid authorization and then used that feature. Please don't leave saying Ethereum was hacked. It wasn't.

## Who can freeze stolen crypto

Some crypto can be frozen and some can't. USDT has Tether behind it and USDC has Circle. Those companies can blacklist specific tokens so they can't be sent or used. Bitcoin has no corporation with a freeze button. When platforms "freeze" Bitcoin, they are really refusing to touch specific coins on their own platform. [Watch at 6:24](?t=384)

Everybody loves censorship resistance until it's their $6 billion that got stolen. So here's my simple question for you: if your money was stolen, would you want it frozen?

This is also why self-custody keeps coming up. Pure Wallet's pitch is that you hold your own funds and use your phone as the security device instead of buying more hardware. I'm not saying that removes risk by magic. I'm saying this hack makes the self-custody conversation a lot more relevant.

## The Binance hole theory

The scary theory going around is that Binance has a hole. Say customers believe they own $100 but Binance only has $90 available. That missing $10 is the hole. Leonidas NFT put it bluntly: the founder of an insolvent exchange will tell users their funds are safe right up until the handcuffs. It is the same thing we heard from FTX. [Watch at 11:06](?t=666)

You need 2 words here. Liquidity means can you pay people right now. Solvency means do you own enough to pay everybody eventually. If everyone tries to withdraw the second it reopens, that is the crypto version of a bank run.

To be clear: I don't think we currently have evidence proving Binance is insolvent. Binance says it set aside over $6.3 billion to cover losses. Saying it and letting people withdraw are 2 different things. The real tests are withdrawals reopening normally and reserves that reconcile. I'm Brazilian. I was born and raised there and moved to the States as a kid, so this one is personal. Brazil held its runoff and Lula won with 51.8% over Flávio Bolsonaro's 48.2%. Here's why people are raising eyebrows. In Brazil, voting is on a Sunday and it's a holiday, so everyone has time to vote.
